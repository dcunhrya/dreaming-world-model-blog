## Introduction

Modern RL has a simple problem: real experience is expensive.

The algorithmic loop is simple enough: take an **action**, **observe** what happens, **update** the policy, repeat. 
But the hardest part is building the world the agent is allowed to learn in. Real environments are expensive, slow, risky, or unavailable. A robot cannot break thousands of dishes. A clinical agent cannot learn by trial and error on patients.

The classical solution is simulation. But simulators are brittle, domain-specific, and only as good as the assumptions we provide.
This is why **world models** have become the buzzword of RL and modern AI. Instead of hand-coding the environment, the agent learns what tends to happen after an action, compresses those dynamics into a predictive model, and then uses that model to generate imagined experience. This is the idea people often call **“dreaming”**.

In this post, we push through the hype. We will define what world models and dreaming actually are, and how imagined rollouts can improve sample efficiency. Then we will build the smallest useful version of the idea: Q-learning in a GridWorld, followed by Dyna-Q with a **learned tabular world model**.
World models started from simple implementations, not the generative models that dominate the headlines today.
