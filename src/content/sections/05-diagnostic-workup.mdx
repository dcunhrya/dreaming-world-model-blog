## A Diagnostic Healthcare World Model

Unlike a fully observable navigation task, a clinical workup is partial information gathering. 
The agent sees only what has been tested so far, not the true diagnosis. At each step it must decide whether to order another test (ECG, troponin, D-dimer, chest X-ray) or commit to a diagnosis, balancing accuracy against test cost and delay. 
Rare but dangerous cases like pulmonary embolism may appear infrequently, which makes real experience scarce.

This section applies **Dyna-Q** to that setting: Q-learning from real simulated patients, plus a learned tabular world model that records what usually happens after each test or diagnosis. 
After every real case, the agent performs extra **imagined updates** from that model. 
Cheap practice on workups it may not see often enough in real rollouts. 
It is an educational toy, not a clinically validated system and **not trained on EHR data**.

<Callout type="warning" title="Synthetic simulator only">
All disease priors and test sensitivities are **hand-specified** approximations inspired by common teaching ranges. They are useful for demonstrating world models and dreaming, not for clinical decision support.
</Callout>

### Clinical question

A patient presents with chest pain. The hidden diagnosis is one of:

| Disease | Role in the toy cohort |
|---------|------------------------|
| **ACS** | Critical  and must not miss |
| **PE** | Critical and often rare |
| **Pneumonia** | Common, testable |
| **Benign cause** | Common, low risk |

The agent can **order tests** (ECG, troponin, D-dimer, chest X-ray) or **commit to a diagnosis**. Each test reveals a positive or negative finding drawn from disease-specific sensitivity tables in the ground-truth simulator.

### Information-state MDP

The **observed state** (what the clinician knows) consists of a risk profile plus which tests have been ordered and their results. 
The hidden disease label is not part of the state. It is a compact information-state approximation to a POMDP (partially observable Markov decision process).

| Component | Definition |
|-----------|------------|
| **Hidden state** | True disease (ACS, PE, pneumonia, benign) |
| **Observed state** s<sub>t</sub> | Risk profile + result history for four tests |
| **Action** a<sub>t</sub> | Order a test or diagnose one of four diseases |
| **Transition** | Test → finding sampled from P(test result \| disease); diagnosis → terminal |
| **Reward** | +correct diagnosis, −wrong diagnosis, extra penalty for missed ACS/PE, −test cost, −delay |

There are 243 tabular information states (3 risk profiles × 3<sup>4</sup> test-result combinations) and 8 actions.

### Ground truth vs learned model

Two layers of simulation:

1. **Ground-truth simulator** — hand-built tables in `src/dreaming/diagnostic/config.ts`. Samples hidden disease and test findings.
2. **Learned world model** — empirical counts of `(s, a) → (s', r, done)` from real rollouts, in the same spirit as tabular Dyna-Q.

Dreaming resamples from the learned model to practice workups the agent rarely sees in real interaction — especially when **PE is uncommon**.
