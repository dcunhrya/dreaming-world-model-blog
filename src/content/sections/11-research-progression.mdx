## Research Progression

The idea is old. Dyna mixed real experience with hypothetical experience from a learned model. World Models showed that neural agents could learn compact latent simulations from pixels. PlaNet and Dreamer moved policy optimization into latent imagination. MuZero showed that a model does not need to predict pixels if it predicts the quantities needed for planning. DreamerV3 and Dreamer4 push the recipe toward broader domains and harder sparse-reward tasks. Genie and V-JEPA show the broader foundation-model version: large models that learn predictive structure from video and interaction.

| Era | Representative work | Core move |
|-----|---------------------|-----------|
| 1990s | Dyna-Q (Sutton, 1990) | Tabular model + imagined Q-updates |
| 2018 | World Models (Ha & Schmidhuber) | Train partly inside a learned latent dream |
| 2019–2025 | Dreamer / DreamerV3 / Dreamer4 | Actor–critic learning in latent rollouts |
| 2020 | MuZero | Model predicts planning-relevant quantities, not pixels |
| 2024–2025 | EnvGen, Genie 3, V-JEPA 2 | Generated or foundation-scale interactive worlds |

In latent world models, imagination happens in a compressed state:

<CodeBlock lang="text" title="Latent dynamics">
{`z_{t+1} ~ p_θ(z_{t+1} | z_t, a_t)`}
</CodeBlock>

The diagnostic workup exercise is the tabular version of that pattern: z<sub>t</sub> is an **information state** over test results rather than a grid cell, with a count-based p<sub>θ</sub>. The frontier adds representation learning, long horizons, and domains where hand-built simulators do not exist.

<Callout type="note" title="Continuity">
Dyna-Q is not a historical curiosity. It is the tabular limit of a design pattern that now appears in Dreamer, MuZero, and emerging foundation world models: **learn consequences, then train inside them**.
</Callout>
