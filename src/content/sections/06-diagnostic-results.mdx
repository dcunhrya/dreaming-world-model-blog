## Diagnostic Workup: Results

We benchmark Q-learning and Dyna-Q (`k ∈ {1, 5, 20, 100}`) against two heuristics on the synthetic chest-pain simulator:

| Baseline | Behavior |
|----------|----------|
| **Prior-only** | Diagnose the highest-prevalence disease without ordering tests |
| **All-tests oracle** | Order every test, then diagnose via simulator posterior |

Each agent trains for 150 episodes × 8 seeds. Metrics include episode return, diagnostic accuracy, macro recall, ACS/PE recall, tests per workup, and missed-critical rate.

### Rare PE prevalence

<DiagnosticResultCharts variant="prevalenceShift" />

When PE prevalence drops, PE recall can collapse even if overall accuracy looks acceptable. Empirical dreaming **cannot invent** disease transitions that never appeared in real rollouts — if PE cases are too rare, the learned model never acquires enough PE-specific test dynamics to dream useful workups.

### Model misspecification (D-dimer bias)

<DiagnosticResultCharts variant="biasFailure" />

We inject bias into the **learned** model by underestimating D-dimer sensitivity during imagined updates. As dream budget increases, return drops — more dreaming amplifies a bad model, and the agent learns to skip PE workups in dreams that look cheaper than reality.

### Cost–safety tradeoff

<DiagnosticResultCharts variant="costSafetyPareto" />

Higher test costs push policies toward fewer orders. The scatter plot tracks **accuracy vs missed ACS/PE rate** as test prices scale — a simple Pareto view of the cost–latency-style tradeoff from systems autoscaling, now in a diagnostic setting.

<Callout type="warning" title="Takeaway">
Dreaming can help when real rare-case experience is scarce and the learned model is accurate enough. In healthcare, the research question is not "generate more patients" — it is whether imagined transitions form a **verified clinical sandbox** before any policy is trusted.
</Callout>

These results are synthetic educational experiments. They demonstrate algorithmic behavior, not evidence for deployment.
