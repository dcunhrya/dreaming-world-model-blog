## What Is a World Model?

"World model" is overloaded. In practice it can mean:

| Type | What it predicts | Example |
|------|------------------|---------|
| Tabular dynamics model | Next state and reward from observed counts | Dyna-Q |
| Neural latent dynamics model | Future latent state and reward | Dreamer |
| Planning model | Reward, value, and policy-relevant latent dynamics | MuZero |
| Interactive generative world | Action-conditioned future observations | Genie, Minecraft world models |

The important idea:

> A world model does not have to perfectly reconstruct the world. It has to preserve enough structure for the agent to choose better actions.

This is related to the **value equivalence principle** (Grimm et al., 2020): a model can be useful if it preserves the quantities that matter for planning, even if it ignores irrelevant details.

<Callout type="tip" title="Memorable line">
A world model is not necessarily a video generator. It is a **consequence model**. It only needs to preserve the parts of the future that matter for action.
</Callout>

In our GridWorld exercise, the world model is deliberately minimal: count-based estimates of P̂(s' | s, a) and R̂(s, a). No pixels, no latent vectors — just enough structure to imagine plausible transitions.
