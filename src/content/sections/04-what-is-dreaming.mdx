## What Is Dreaming?

Dreaming is imagined rollout inside a learned model:

<CodeBlock lang="text" title="Dream step">
{`start from known state s
choose action a (from memory or policy)
sample predicted next state s' ~ P_hat(· | s, a)
sample predicted reward r ~ R_hat(s, a)
update Q / policy / critic
repeat`}
</CodeBlock>

The full loop in model-based RL looks like this:

<CodeBlock lang="text" title="Model-based RL loop">
{`real env step
    ↓
update world model
    ↓
sample imagined transitions
    ↓
update Q / policy / critic
    ↓
act again`}
</CodeBlock>

<Callout type="tip" title="Train-time vs test-time compute">
**Test-time compute** asks: can the model think longer before acting?

**Train-time dreaming** asks: can the model cheaply generate more experience before deployment?
</Callout>

<Callout type="note" title="The train-time compute knob">
**No dreaming:** 1 real transition = 1 learning update

**Dreaming with k = 20:** 1 real transition = 1 real update + 20 imagined updates
</Callout>

Dreaming is not free. Each imagined update costs compute. But imagined updates do not cost **real** interaction — and real interaction is often the bottleneck. That tradeoff is the central systems question: when is it worth spending train-time compute to simulate experience you have not physically collected?
